From the President

Welcome to the first newsletter of the Society of Natural Capital Accounting. We plan to publish it monthly, on the first Tuesday of the month, and to keep it short. In our foundation year, it is free and open to all.

The Society was launched online on 18 August. Since then we have opened membership, held our first seminar and set up a way for anyone to propose a seminar. We have also had a couple of special features by John Finisdore and myself (see Society news).

The theme of the first newsletter is the breadth of the field. Our first seminar compared the natural capital accounting frameworks used in the private sector with each other and with the System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA). A new review counts more than 165 environmental accounting frameworks and is discussed below. Each month statistics offices and central banks release new accounts, academic papers appear, and organisations release guidelines and frameworks. Some of this is highlighted below, and such highlights will be included in future newsletters.

This breadth is why the Society views natural capital accounting as one profession, practised in many settings. The concepts are largely shared. What differs is scope, purpose and users. Understanding those differences, and being able to work across them, is part of being a natural capital accountant.

Two things to do this month. If you have not yet joined, the early discount on foundation membership closes on 30 November. And if you have an idea for a seminar, a feature or an item for this newsletter, please send it to us.

Michael Vardon, President

Highlights

Ecosystem accounts are becoming routine. Ireland's Central Statistics Office (CSO) released ecosystem extent accounts for 2024, showing change since 2018: built-up land up 10.1 per cent and broadleaved forest up 5.1 per cent (about 7,400 hectares). Brazil's statistics office (IBGE) published experimental ecosystem accounts for protected areas in Rio de Janeiro and the Caatinga. EU Member States must send their first ecosystem extent accounts, on 2024 data, to Eurostat by the end of 2026, so more are on the way.

Energy in Australia. The Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Energy Account for 2024–25 shows net energy use down 2.8 per cent to 23,037 petajoules, and renewables supplying 37 per cent of domestic electricity use.

Emissions at the pace of GDP. Statistics Sweden publishes air emissions accounts each quarter, alongside GDP. In the second quarter of 2026 emissions fell 1.5 per cent while GDP grew 3.8 per cent. Spain released advance air emission accounts for 2025 within ten months of the end of the year.

Recent papers

Each month the newsletter will list the most relevant papers and highlight one. A Scopus search and AI tools (Claude and ChatGPT) will be used to screen and assess papers, and a person then selects the papers listed.

Mapping the frameworks, then getting them to talk to each other. How many environmental accounting frameworks are there? A new open access review started with more than 165 frameworks and judged 23 to be operationally relevant, from the SEEA through biodiversity assessments to corporate reporting and financial stability frameworks. The authors argue that the field needs existing frameworks to share data, through common classifications, metadata and machine-readable standards, rather than another framework. It picks up where our first seminar left off: the seminar compared frameworks side by side, and the paper asks what it would take for them to work together.

Bulckaen, A., Iguarta Lopez, J.I., Villa, F. and Ibarra Zuluaga, D. (2026) 'A critical review of environmental accounting frameworks: Toward an operational harmonisation agenda', Ecosystem Services, 82, 101928. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoser.2026.101928

Standards and frameworks

SEEA Central Framework update. The third and last tranche of the global consultation on guidance notes is scheduled for October and November and was listed as "coming soon" on 7 November. Its nine notes include the links between the Central Framework and SEEA Ecosystem Accounting, the carbon stock account, valuation of water and treatment of the atmosphere as an asset. The summary report and responses for the first tranche are published; the report on the second tranche, which closed on 7 August, is to come. Consultation page.

Corporate nature disclosure. On 22 October the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) published an Exposure Draft of IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Practice Statement 1, Nature-related Disclosures, which builds on the framework of the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (TNFD). Comments close on 19 February 2027. On 13 October the TNFD released version 2.0 of its LEAP approach and updated recommendations, adding guidance on measuring the state of nature. Much of what these ask companies to report is the kind of information accounts provide.

The London Group in Guatemala. The 32nd meeting of the London Group on Environmental Accounting was held in Guatemala City from 7 to 9 September. A week earlier, the Banco de Guatemala presented its first integrated environmental-economic accounts, covering forests, water, energy and emissions, waste and material flows from 2013 to 2024, with annual updates planned to 2030.
